Lesson 9 of 10

How Smart Are Elephants

🤔 Guess First!

  1. Can you name another animal (besides humans) that can recognize itself in a mirror?
  2. Do you think elephants can feel sad, or is that just a human thing?
  3. Do you think elephants can use tools, the way some birds and monkeys do?

🐘 The Big Idea

Elephants are some of the smartest animals on the planet — they have huge brains, amazing memories, can recognize themselves in a mirror, use tools, and even appear to grieve when a family member dies.

📖 Let's Learn More

An elephant's brain weighs around 5 kg (11 lbs) — bigger than any other land animal's brain — packed with regions for memory, learning, and emotion. That big brain shows up in some remarkable behavior:

Mirror self-recognition: Scientists tested an Asian elephant by putting a mark on its head that it could only see in a mirror. The elephant touched the mark on its own head after looking in the mirror — proof it understood the reflection was *itself*, not another elephant. Only a small handful of animals have passed this test: elephants, great apes (like chimpanzees), dolphins, and magpies.

Tool use: Elephants have been seen using branches to swat flies, dropping objects on electric fences to test if they're on, and even modifying branches to make better fly-swatters.

Empathy and grief: Elephants comfort distressed herd members by touching them gently with their trunks and making soothing rumbles. When a herd member dies, especially a matriarch, other elephants have been seen gently touching the body with their trunks, standing quietly nearby — sometimes for days — and even returning to visit the bones long afterward. Scientists have documented herds returning to a matriarch's remains almost two years after she died.

🤯 Cool Facts

  • Elephants can recognize and remember dozens, maybe hundreds, of other individual elephants across many years.
  • A matriarch's memory of danger, water, and migration routes is passed down and taught to younger elephants — a kind of elephant "culture."
  • Elephants have shown they understand when their own body is in the way of solving a puzzle — a very advanced kind of self-awareness for an animal.

❓ Quiz Time

Q1. What is the mirror test, and what did it show about elephants?

Scientists put an unusual mark on an elephant where it could only see it in a mirror; the elephant touched its own head, showing it recognized the reflection as itself.

Q2. Name one way elephants use tools.

Using branches to swat flies (or modifying branches into better fly-swatters, or testing objects on electric fences).

Q3. What do elephants sometimes do when a family member dies?

Gently touch the body with their trunks, stand quietly nearby (sometimes for days), and may even return to visit the remains long afterward.

🎨 Try This!

Next time you're near a mirror, try the "mirror test" idea on yourself — put a sticker somewhere on your face you can't see directly (like your forehead), look in the mirror, and see if you reach for it. That's basically what scientists tested with the elephant!

🗣️ Explain It!

Explain to a friend why passing the mirror test is a big deal — why does it matter that an animal understands "that's ME in the mirror"?

📚 New Words

Self-recognition
understanding that a reflection or image is of yourself, not another creature.
Empathy
noticing and caring about what another individual is feeling.
Grief
the sadness and mourning felt after losing someone.
