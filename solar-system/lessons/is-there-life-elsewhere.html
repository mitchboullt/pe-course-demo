Is There Life Elsewhere

🔭 Predict First

  1. Name one place in our own solar system (besides Earth) that you think is a serious candidate for hosting life.
  2. Do you think scientists need to find sunlight for life to exist somewhere, or can life survive without it?
  3. What do you think a "biosignature" is?

🪐 The Big Picture

The search for life beyond Earth has two active fronts: promising ocean worlds right here in our own solar system, and thousands of confirmed planets orbiting other stars — some of which have already produced tantalizing, still-debated hints of possible biosignatures.

🔬 How It Works

For decades, the search for life focused on the habitable zone — the range of distances from a star where a planet's temperature could allow liquid water to exist on its surface (not too hot, not too cold). Earth sits comfortably in the Sun's habitable zone, and this framework is still useful for scanning distant star systems. But discoveries within our own solar system have complicated the simple picture: Jupiter's moon Europa and Saturn's moon Enceladus both appear to have liquid water oceans beneath thick ice shells, kept liquid not by sunlight (which barely reaches that far out) but by internal heat generated through tidal flexing — the repeated gravitational stretching and squeezing from their giant parent planets. This shows that liquid water — and possibly habitable environments — can exist far outside the traditional habitable zone, as long as there's another heat source.

Beyond our own solar system, astronomers have confirmed thousands of exoplanets orbiting other stars, largely using missions like Kepler and TESS, which detect the tiny dimming of a star's light as a planet passes in front of it. The James Webb Space Telescope can now analyze the atmospheres of some of these worlds by studying starlight filtering through them, searching for biosignatures — chemical signs (like unusual combinations of gases) that are difficult to explain without biological activity. One such candidate, the exoplanet K2-18b, has generated significant excitement (and significant scientific caution) after detections of a gas called dimethyl sulfide, which on Earth is produced almost exclusively by living organisms — but as of 2026, the scientific community still debates whether the detection is solid and whether non-biological explanations have been fully ruled out. This is a healthy, normal part of how extraordinary claims get tested in science: replication and skepticism before consensus.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

NASA's Europa Clipper (launched 2024) and ESA's JUICE mission (launched 2023) are both en route to Jupiter's icy moons right now, to study Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto up close — while Dragonfly, a nuclear-powered drone-like lander, is scheduled to explore Saturn's moon Titan starting in 2028, investigating the prebiotic chemistry in its methane lakes.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Confirmed exoplanets discovered so far: well over 5,000.
  • Europa and Enceladus are both thought to have oceans containing more liquid water than all of Earth's oceans combined.
  • K2-18b is roughly 8.6x Earth's mass, orbiting a red dwarf star about 124 light-years away.

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "A planet needs direct sunlight reaching a liquid ocean for life to be possible there."** Europa and Enceladus show that tidal heating can keep water liquid far from the Sun, opening up habitability possibilities well outside the traditional habitable zone.
  • "Scientists have found alien life."** As of 2026, no confirmed detection of extraterrestrial life exists — what's been found are intriguing, still-debated candidate biosignatures, which is a meaningfully different and much more cautious claim.

Q1. What is the "habitable zone"?

Q2. How do Europa and Enceladus keep water liquid despite being far from the Sun?

Q3. What is a biosignature?

📚 New Terms

Habitable zone
the range of distances from a star where liquid water could exist on a planet's surface.
Tidal heating
internal heat generated by repeated gravitational flexing from a nearby massive body.
Exoplanet
a planet orbiting a star other than the Sun.
Biosignature
a chemical or physical sign that's difficult to explain without biological activity.

🔗 Related Concepts

  • Mars — the search for past life closer to home.
  • Jupiter — home of Europa, one of the leading ocean-world candidates.
  • Saturn — home of Enceladus and Titan, two more astrobiology targets.
