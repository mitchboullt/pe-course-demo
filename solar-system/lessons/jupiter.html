Jupiter

🔭 Predict First

  1. Roughly how many Earths do you think could fit inside Jupiter?
  2. Jupiter is the biggest planet — do you think it also rotates the slowest, or the fastest?
  3. What do you think the Great Red Spot actually is?

🪐 The Big Picture

Jupiter is the largest planet by far — more massive than every other planet in the solar system combined — and it rotates faster than any of them, with a storm bigger than Earth that's been raging for centuries.

🔬 How It Works

Jupiter is a gas giant, made almost entirely of hydrogen and helium with no solid surface to stand on — what looks like a surface in photos is just the top of a very deep atmosphere. Despite its enormous size, Jupiter has the fastest rotation of any planet, spinning once in under 10 hours. That rapid spin flattens the planet slightly (it's visibly wider at the equator than pole-to-pole) and whips its atmosphere into distinct colored bands of clouds moving in opposite directions at different latitudes.

The Great Red Spot is a giant, persistent storm — a rotating high-pressure system big enough to swallow Earth whole — that's been observed for at least 150 years, and possibly much longer. Nobody fully understands why it has lasted so long when storms on Earth dissipate in days or weeks; the leading explanation involves Jupiter's lack of a solid surface to create the friction that would normally slow a storm down.

Jupiter has an enormous gravitational influence on the rest of the solar system — it's massive enough to noticeably affect the orbits of other bodies, and it's the reason the asteroid belt never became a planet (see the previous note). It has over 100 confirmed moons, including the four large Galilean moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto) discovered by Galileo in 1610 — Ganymede is actually larger than the planet Mercury, and Europa is one of the most promising places in the solar system to search for life, thanks to a suspected liquid water ocean beneath its icy shell.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

NASA's Europa Clipper, launched in 2024, is on its way to Jupiter to perform dozens of close flybys of Europa — mapping its icy shell and analyzing material from possible plumes erupting from its subsurface ocean, specifically searching for conditions that could support life.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Distance from Sun: 5.2 AU
  • Orbital period (year): ~11.86 Earth years
  • Rotation period (day): under 10 hours — fastest of any planet
  • Diameter: 139,820 km — about 11x Earth's diameter
  • Mass: more than 2.5x the mass of all other planets combined
  • Moons: over 100 confirmed, including 4 large Galilean moons

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "Jupiter has a solid surface somewhere deep down, like a rocky planet."** It doesn't — it's gas and, deeper still, exotic states of hydrogen, all the way to a poorly-understood core; there's no solid "ground" to land on.
  • "Bigger planet means slower rotation."** The opposite is true here — Jupiter is the biggest planet and also spins the fastest of all eight.

❓ Test Yourself

Q1. What is Jupiter mostly made of?

Hydrogen and helium gas — it has no solid surface.

Q2. How does Jupiter's rotation speed compare to the other planets?

It's the fastest — Jupiter completes one rotation in under 10 hours, despite being the largest planet.

Q3. Why is Europa considered a strong candidate in the search for life?

It's thought to have a liquid water ocean beneath its icy shell, one of the key ingredients scientists look for.

📚 New Terms

Gas giant
a large planet made mostly of hydrogen and helium, without a solid surface.
Galilean moons
Jupiter's four largest moons (Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto), discovered by Galileo in 1610.

🔗 Related Concepts

  • The Asteroid Belt — shaped (and prevented from becoming a planet) by Jupiter's gravity.
  • Saturn — the second gas giant, and famous for a very different feature.
