The Sun

🔭 Predict First

  1. What do you think powers the Sun — burning like a fire, or something else entirely?

  3. About how many Earths do you think could fit inside the Sun?

🪐 The Big Picture

The Sun is a massive ball of superheated plasma held together by its own gravity, powered by nuclear fusion in its core — and it's the reason every planet in the solar system exists where it does and (on Earth, at least) the reason life is possible at all.

🔬 How It Works

The Sun isn't burning in the way a fire burns — there's no oxygen involved. Instead, deep in its core, where temperatures reach about 15 million °C and pressure is crushing, hydrogen nuclei are forced together so violently that they fuse into helium. This process, nuclear fusion, converts a tiny fraction of the mass involved directly into energy (following Einstein's famous E = mc²), and it's this energy — released as light and heat — that eventually makes its way to the surface and out into space, including the sliver that reaches Earth and powers essentially all life here.

The Sun has layers. Energy from the core travels outward through the radiative zone, where photons bounce around chaotically — a single photon can take tens of thousands of years just to random-walk its way out of this zone. Above that is the convective zone, where hot plasma physically rises, cools, and sinks in slow-motion currents, similar to boiling water, carrying energy the rest of the way to the visible surface (the photosphere). Beyond the visible surface is the Sun's outer atmosphere, the corona — and this is where things get genuinely strange: the corona is far hotter than the surface below it, reaching 1–2 million °C, even though it's farther from the fusion happening in the core. Scientists are still refining exactly how the corona gets so much hotter than the surface it sits above; leading explanations involve magnetic energy being released into the thin coronal plasma.

The Sun also constantly sheds material and energy outward as the solar wind — a stream of charged particles that flows past every planet in the solar system, and is what shapes comet tails and interacts with Earth's magnetic field to create auroras.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

NASA's Parker Solar Probe, launched in 2018, is the first spacecraft to fly directly through the Sun's corona, using a heat shield to survive temperatures the spacecraft body itself would never withstand unprotected. It's been gathering direct measurements to help solve the long-standing mystery of why the corona is so much hotter than the surface beneath it.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Core temperature: about 15 million °C.
  • Surface (photosphere) temperature: about 5,500 °C.
  • Corona temperature: 1–2 million °C — hotter than the surface, despite being farther from the core.
  • About 1.3 million Earths could fit inside the Sun by volume.
  • The Sun converts roughly 4 million tonnes of mass into energy every second through fusion.

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "The Sun is on fire."** Fire is a chemical reaction (combustion) that needs oxygen; the Sun's energy comes from nuclear fusion, a completely different physical process happening under extreme pressure and heat, with no burning involved.
  • "The corona should be cooler than the surface, since it's farther from the core."** Counterintuitively, it's much hotter — one of the genuine unsolved puzzles in solar physics, not just a simple "farther = cooler" situation.

❓ Test Yourself

Q1. What process actually powers the Sun?

Nuclear fusion — hydrogen nuclei fusing into helium in the core, releasing energy.

Q2. Which is hotter: the Sun's visible surface, or its corona?

The corona (1-2 million °C) is hotter than the surface (about 5,500 °C) — a genuine open puzzle in solar physics.

Q3. What is the solar wind?

A constant stream of charged particles flowing outward from the Sun through the whole solar system.

📚 New Terms

Nuclear fusion
forcing atomic nuclei together so hard they combine into a heavier nucleus, releasing energy.
Photosphere
the visible "surface" of the Sun.
Corona
the Sun's extremely hot outer atmosphere.
Solar wind
the constant outward stream of charged particles from the Sun.
