Venus

🪐 The Big Picture

Venus is almost exactly Earth's size and often called Earth's twin — but it's also the hottest planet in the solar system and one of the most hostile places imaginable, thanks to a runaway greenhouse effect.

🔬 How It Works

Venus and Earth are similar in size, mass, and composition, which is why "twin" gets used — but their surfaces couldn't be more different. Venus's atmosphere is almost entirely carbon dioxide, at a surface pressure about 90 times Earth's — comparable to the crushing pressure nearly a kilometer underwater. Thick clouds of sulfuric acid trap heat so effectively that Venus's surface temperature reaches about 465°C, hot enough to melt lead — and unlike Mercury, this heat barely varies between day and night, or between the equator and poles, because the thick atmosphere spreads it around so efficiently. This is the greenhouse effect taken to an extreme: the same basic physics that keeps Earth's climate livable, scaled up until it becomes a planetary furnace.

Venus's rotation is also strange in its own way: it spins backward compared to most planets (a property called retrograde rotation), and extremely slowly — one Venus day (243 Earth days) is actually longer than one Venus year (225 Earth days). Nobody knows for certain why Venus rotates backward; a giant ancient collision is one leading hypothesis.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

The Soviet Venera program achieved something no other mission has repeated: landing spacecraft on Venus's surface and transmitting images back, in the 1970s and 80s — the landers survived only a couple of hours before the heat and pressure destroyed them, but they remain the only photographs ever taken from the surface of another planet's surface this hostile.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Distance from Sun: 0.72 AU
  • Orbital period (year): 225 Earth days
  • Rotation period (day): 243 Earth days — longer than its year, and backward (retrograde)
  • Surface temperature: ~465°C (hottest planet in the solar system)
  • Atmospheric pressure at surface: ~90x Earth's
  • Moons: 0

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "Venus is Earth's twin, so it must be somewhat similar to live on."** Similar in size only — the surface is utterly lethal, with crushing pressure, sulfuric acid clouds, and a temperature that melts lead.
  • "The greenhouse effect is inherently bad."** The same basic physics keeps Earth's average temperature livable rather than frozen — Venus shows what happens when that effect runs away unchecked, which is a caution about degree, not a sign that the greenhouse effect itself is a flaw.

❓ Test Yourself

Q1. Why is Venus hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun?

Venus's thick CO2 atmosphere traps heat extremely effectively (a runaway greenhouse effect), while Mercury has essentially no atmosphere to trap any heat at all.

Q2. How does Venus's day length compare to its year length?

A Venus day (243 Earth days) is longer than a Venus year (225 Earth days).

Q3. What's unusual about the direction Venus rotates?

It rotates backward (retrograde) compared to most planets, including Earth.

🗣️ Explain It

Explain to a friend how the same greenhouse effect that helps make Earth livable can, taken to an extreme, make a planet nearly the same size into the hottest place in the solar system.

📚 New Terms

Greenhouse effect
when an atmosphere traps heat from sunlight, warming a planet's surface.
Retrograde rotation
spinning in the opposite direction from most other bodies in the solar system.

🔗 Related Concepts

  • Mercury — closer to the Sun, but far cooler on average than Venus.
