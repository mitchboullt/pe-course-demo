Mercury

🔭 Predict First

  1. Mercury is closest to the Sun — do you think it's also the hottest planet?
  2. Does Mercury have a longer day (one full rotation) or a longer year (one full orbit)?
  3. Does Mercury have any moons?

🪐 The Big Picture

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun — a cratered, airless world with the most extreme temperature swings of any planet, and one genuinely surprising fact: it's not actually the hottest.

🔬 How It Works

Mercury orbits at just 0.39 AU, completing a full trip around the Sun in only 88 Earth days — the shortest year in the solar system. But its rotation is bizarre: a single Mercury day (from sunrise to sunrise) takes about 176 Earth days — longer than two of its own years. Mercury is trapped in a 3:2 spin-orbit resonance, rotating exactly three times for every two orbits, a gravitational lock caused by the Sun's tidal pull on Mercury's slightly elongated shape.

Because Mercury has essentially no atmosphere to trap or spread heat, its temperature swings are the most extreme of any planet: up to 430°C on the sunlit side, and down to -180°C on the night side — more than a 600-degree swing on the same planet. This is exactly why Mercury isn't the hottest planet overall (that title goes to Venus, see the next note): Mercury has no blanket of atmosphere to hold heat in, so it loses it all rapidly once the Sun isn't directly overhead.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft orbited Mercury from 2011–2015 and made a striking discovery: permanently shadowed craters near Mercury's poles — despite the planet's blistering daytime heat — contain water ice, protected because sunlight never reaches the crater floors.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Distance from Sun: 0.39 AU
  • Orbital period (year): 88 Earth days
  • Rotation period (day): ~176 Earth days
  • Diameter: 4,879 km (smaller than Jupiter's moon Ganymede)
  • Moons: 0
  • Temperature range: -180°C to 430°C

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "Mercury is the hottest planet since it's closest to the Sun."** It's actually Venus (next note) — Mercury has no atmosphere to trap heat, so despite blistering daytime temperatures, it cools drastically at night.
  • "A planet's 'day' and 'year' are unrelated in a simple way."** Mercury shows just how tangled they can get — its day is longer than half of its own year, a direct result of its gravitational lock with the Sun.

❓ Test Yourself

Q1. Why isn't Mercury the hottest planet, despite being closest to the Sun?

It has essentially no atmosphere to trap heat, so its nightside temperature plummets — its average temperature is much lower than a planet (like Venus) that traps heat with a thick atmosphere.

Q2. How does Mercury's day length compare to its year length?

A Mercury day (~176 Earth days) is longer than a Mercury year (~88 Earth days).

Q3. What did MESSENGER discover in Mercury's polar craters?

Water ice, preserved in permanently shadowed crater floors that sunlight never reaches.

📚 New Terms

Spin-orbit resonance
a fixed ratio between how fast a body rotates and how fast it orbits, locked in by gravity.
Tidal pull
the stretching gravitational effect one body has on another, strongest at close range.
