Mars

🔭 Predict First

  1. Why do you think Mars looks reddish in the sky and in photos?

  3. Does Mars have moons? If so, how many do you think?

🪐 The Big Picture

Mars is the "Red Planet" — cold, dry, and thin-aired today — but overwhelming evidence shows it once had rivers, lakes, and maybe even oceans, making it one of the best places in the solar system to search for signs of past life.

🔬 How It Works

Mars looks red because its surface is covered in iron oxide — essentially rust — formed when iron-rich minerals reacted with oxygen (and possibly ancient water) long ago. Its atmosphere today is extremely thin, only about 0.6% of Earth's pressure, and made mostly of carbon dioxide. That thin atmosphere can't trap much heat or shield the surface, so temperatures average around -63°C, though they can swing from about 20°C at the equator midday to below -120°C at the poles.

Mars wasn't always like this. Rovers and orbiters have found unmistakable geological evidence — dry riverbeds, mineral deposits that only form in liquid water, ancient lakebeds — showing that billions of years ago Mars had a thicker atmosphere and flowing liquid water on its surface. Scientists think Mars lost most of its atmosphere because, unlike Earth, it lacks a strong global magnetic field: without that protective magnetic shield, the solar wind gradually stripped the atmosphere away over billions of years, and as pressure and temperature dropped, surface water could no longer stay liquid.

Mars has two small, oddly-shaped moons, Phobos and Deimos — most scientists think they're captured asteroids rather than moons that formed alongside Mars, based on their small size, irregular shape, and composition.

🚀 Mission Spotlight

NASA's Perseverance rover, which landed in 2021, is actively collecting rock samples in Jezero Crater — an ancient lakebed — specifically designed to be picked up by a future mission and returned to Earth for lab analysis, in the ongoing search for possible biosignatures from when Mars was wetter.

📊 By The Numbers

  • Distance from Sun: 1.52 AU
  • Orbital period (year): 687 Earth days (~1.9 years)
  • Rotation period (day): ~24.6 hours — very close to Earth's
  • Diameter: 6,779 km (about half of Earth's)
  • Moons: 2 (Phobos and Deimos, likely captured asteroids)
  • Average temperature: -63°C

❗ Common Misconceptions

  • "Mars has always been the barren desert we see today."** Strong geological evidence shows ancient Mars had flowing liquid water and a thicker atmosphere — it changed dramatically over billions of years.
  • "Finding evidence of past water on Mars means finding past life."** They're related questions but not the same thing — liquid water is one requirement scientists look for, not proof of life on its own.

📚 New Terms

Iron oxide
rust; the compound that gives Mars its reddish color.
Global magnetic field
a planet-wide magnetic shield (generated by a molten metal core) that can protect an atmosphere from being stripped by the solar wind.
Biosignature
a substance or pattern that provides scientific evidence of past or present life.

🔗 Related Concepts

  • Earth and the Moon — the planet that kept its magnetic field, atmosphere, and surface water.
  • The Asteroid Belt — the neighborhood Mars's captured moons likely came from.
